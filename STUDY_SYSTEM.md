# Study System

## Objective

Build durable economics mastery from principles through intermediate economics while succeeding in current Fall 2026 courses:

- Industrial Economics / Industrial Organization
- Development Economics
- Intro to Econometrics with Python
- Economics Seminar
- Construction Management
- Work obligations
- Christian discipleship and study of God

The system should reduce executive-function load rather than depend on perfect motivation or memory.

## Core methodology

Each serious lesson should usually follow this sequence:

1. **Cold retrieval** - 2 to 5 questions from prior material without notes.
2. **Diagnose the failure** - distinguish concept, math, graph, vocabulary, memory, or causal-reasoning errors.
3. **Intuition first** - explain the economic mechanism in plain language.
4. **Real-world anchor** - connect it to construction, business, policy, history, or the current course/project.
5. **Model** - introduce the economist's abstraction.
6. **Math/graph** - only the mathematics needed for the concept; translate notation into English.
7. **Worked example** - fully explained.
8. **Guided attempt** - partial scaffolding.
9. **Independent attempt** - no help.
10. **Transfer** - change the setting so recognition alone is insufficient.
11. **Compression** - retain only the few facts/equations/mechanisms worth carrying forward.
12. **Spaced return** - bring the concept back after intervening material.

## What counts as mastery

Do not mark a concept mastered because it made sense while visible.

Target: **Can the learner reconstruct and use it later without help?**

Evidence of stronger mastery:

- explains it in plain English
- solves a fresh example
- interprets the graph/equation
- recognizes the concept in a different domain
- distinguishes it from a tempting wrong concept
- retrieves it after days or weeks

## Three simultaneous layers

### 1. Course-survival layer
What is due next and what professors are currently teaching.

### 2. Economics-mastery layer
Principles -> intermediate economics, reordered to support current classes instead of blindly following textbook order.

### 3. Retention layer
Short cumulative retrieval so older math/economics does not disappear.

## Normal session structure

Typical serious session:

- **5-10 min** cumulative retrieval
- **25-40 min** new/current economics concept
- **15-30 min** application to the most relevant class/project
- **2-5 min** compression and checkpoint

Session length may expand or shrink. The structure matters more than a fixed timer.

## Weekly cadence

Starting default, to be adjusted around real deadlines and work:

- Main economics teaching: **3-4x/week**
- Econometrics/Python: **2x/week**
- Seminar research/writing: **1-2x/week**
- Development-specific review: **1-2x/week**
- IO project progress: **at least 1x/week**
- Construction catch-up/planning: **1x/week minimum**
- Semester reprioritization: **weekly, ~15 min**
- Scripture/prayer/discipleship: **short daily continuity**, plus longer study/community/worship as appropriate

Deadlines determine emphasis; mastery determines what gets repaired.

## Overloaded-day rule

A short continuity session is acceptable on unusually overloaded days, but it is not a substitute for projects, assignments, or exam preparation.

Minimum useful continuity:

- a few retrieval questions
- one small next action on the nearest obligation
- short Scripture/prayer

Avoid all-or-nothing failure modes.

## Math policy

Do not restart all mathematics from scratch unless evidence demands it.

Maintain fundamentals continuously underneath economics:

- fractions/decimals/percentages
- ratios and percent change
- algebra
- functions
- graph slope/intercepts
- exponents/logs
- probability/statistics
- derivatives and optimization when needed

Use economics to give math meaning. Reintroduce old math periodically so it remains retrievable.

## ADHD / executive-function policy

Use ADHD-compatible design without assuming every difficulty is caused by ADHD.

Prefer:

- one visible next action
- low setup friction
- externalized deadlines
- explicit resume points
- smaller task boundaries
- frequent retrieval
- flexible session duration
- limited number of study surfaces

Avoid:

- vague tasks like "study chapter 4"
- maintaining several competing planners
- making the study system itself into a large project
- depending on motivation to remember what to do next

## Writing methodology

For Seminar and research projects:

Question -> mechanism/theory -> literature -> evidence/data -> method/identification -> interpretation -> limitations -> conclusion.

Develop large papers incrementally rather than in one end-loaded writing sprint.

## Econometrics methodology

Learn econometrics as reasoning about evidence, not Python syntax memorization.

Question -> data -> cleaning -> visualization -> model -> estimation -> interpretation -> causal challenge -> limitations.

Core distinction to reinforce repeatedly:

**regression/association does not by itself prove causation.**

## Discipleship approach

Keep discipleship connected to life rather than making it another graded productivity system.

Simple recurring frame:

1. Read Scripture
2. What does this reveal about God?
3. What does this reveal about humanity/me?
4. What command, principle, promise, warning, or invitation is present?
5. What concrete act of obedience/love follows?
6. Pray

Keep empirical and moral questions distinct:

- economics can help estimate consequences and tradeoffs
- Christian theology/ethics addresses what ends are good and how a disciple should live
- faith should not be used to falsify empirical evidence
- economic efficiency alone does not establish moral goodness

## Repo storage rule

Store only durable state:

- mastery changes
- course requirements/deadlines
- AI-policy constraints
- project decisions
- recurring weaknesses
- major methodology changes
- current checkpoint

Do not store routine conversation transcripts or every solved practice question.
