# Industrial Organization Project

## Purpose

Use the semester industry project as an applied laboratory for Industrial Organization concepts rather than treating it as separate busywork.

## Durable project structure

The archived project sequence indicates:

1. Group formation / industry selection
2. Research Part I
3. Research Part II
4. Preliminary industry assessment + detailed outline
5. First draft / draft-development step (referenced later, but the uploaded archive did not include the full Step 5 rubric)
6. Presentation
7. Final draft

Do not invent missing Step 5 details. Verify the current Canvas rubric before using weights or requirements for that step.

## Research Part I

At least 3 credible sources.

For each source:
- summarize what it finds
- explain why it matters economically
- connect it to possible IO mechanisms
- evaluate source credibility and limitations

Analysis matters more than article summary.

## Research Part II

At least 3 additional credible sources.

Shift from isolated summaries to synthesis:
- where sources agree
- where they conflict
- whether evidence varies by geography, time, product segment, or market definition
- what this implies for market power or competitive conditions

## Preliminary industry assessment / outline

Produce a 1-2 page preliminary assessment plus a detailed outline.

Use the IO lens below to organize the analysis.

## IO Lens

For the chosen industry, ask:

| Concept | Applied question |
|---|---|
| Demand | Who buys and what shifts demand? |
| Elasticity | How sensitive are customers to price? |
| Costs | What are the important fixed and variable costs? |
| Market structure | How many meaningful competitors exist? |
| Concentration | How concentrated is the relevant market? |
| Market power | Can firms raise price without losing too many buyers? |
| Product differentiation | Why choose one seller/product over another? |
| Price discrimination | Do different customers effectively pay different prices? |
| Oligopoly | Do major firms react strategically to one another? |
| Entry barriers | What prevents or slows new entry? |
| Entry deterrence | Can incumbents make entry less attractive? |
| Collusion | Could coordination be created and sustained? |
| Mergers | Why combine firms, and how might competition change? |
| Antitrust | What conduct or structure could attract scrutiny? |
| Regulation | How does government alter prices, entry, quality, or conduct? |

## Important analytical rule

Absence of a phenomenon is not a blank section. Explain why it may be absent.

Example: if collusion is not evident, investigate whether there are too many firms, unstable demand, heterogeneous products, difficult monitoring, easy entry, or strong incentives to undercut.

## AI-use constraints

Course policy permits certain AI research/writing-support uses, but:
- verify claims and sources independently
- the economic analysis must be the student's own
- every group member must be able to explain any part of the project
- final submission requires a 100-150 word AI-use disclosure/reflection even if AI was not used

Use AI primarily as tutor, critic, brainstorming partner, structure checker, and editor rather than as a substitute analyst.
